## What it is

Rob Zelinka, a technology leader in financial services, discusses data center infrastructure bottlenecks created by AI workloads. He highlights the jump from traditional 10kW per rack to 50-100kW baselines for AI, forcing facility assessments of electrical capacity, thermal management transitions from air to liquid cooling, and escalating brownfield retrofit costs.

## Why it matters

The 5-10x increase in rack power density (10kW to 50-100kW) for AI workloads forces facilities teams to validate whether existing electrical distribution can support new GPU clusters before deployment. Brownfield sites face a 'retrofit tax' of upgraded power delivery and cooling infrastructure, turning AI adoption into an industrial engineering project that affects capital budgets, timelines, and site selection decisions.

**Evidence from source:**

- Power density moving from traditional 10kW per rack to 50-100kW baseline for AI workloads
- Question posed: 'can our current facility even pull the juice required for a new H100 cluster?'
- Brownfield retrofit costs 'skyrocketing' with explicit mention of 'Retrofit tax' for upgrading existing sites
