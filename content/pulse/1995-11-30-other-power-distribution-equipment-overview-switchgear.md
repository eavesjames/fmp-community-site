## What it is

EC&M article from 1995 describes power distribution equipment categories including switchgear, switchboards, panelboards, and transfer switches. Covers medium voltage (4.16-34.5kV) and low voltage equipment used to segment utility or generator power into smaller blocks for end-use loads in residential, commercial, and industrial settings.

## Why it matters

Provides foundational taxonomy of distribution equipment and voltage transformation steps, but the excerpt contains no specific constraints, failure modes, project contexts, or code references. Electrical contractors and facilities teams need concrete details—panel sizing, breaker coordination, AHJ requirements—not general definitions to inform design or install decisions.

**Evidence from source:**

- Residential/small commercial served at 120/240V single-phase to 480Y/277V 3-phase; larger users at 4.16, 13.8, or 34.5kV medium voltage
- Medium voltage subdivided by metalclad switchgear using SF6 or air circuit breakers, or metal-enclosed switchgear with fused switches
- Power transformed at unit substations near major loads, then distributed via switchgear to motor control centers and panelboards
